# Semi-Supervised Medical Image Segmentation via Foundation Model-Guided Priors and Shapley-Based Dual-Teacher Learning
## Abstract
Semi-supervised medical image segmentation aims to leverage abundant unlabeled images to reduce the dependence on costly pixel-wise annotations. Although recent foundation models have demonstrated promising cross-domain representation and generalization capability, directly applying vision-language models and segmentation foundation models to medical image segmentation often results in inaccurate lesion localization and unreliable pseudo-supervision. To address this issue, we propose FPDT-Net, a two-stage semi-supervised framework driven by foundation model-guided priors. In Stage I, lesion-related textual descriptions are introduced to fine-tune BiomedCLIP through an Adaptive Ranked Hard Negative Decoupled Contrastive Learning (ARHN-DCL) objective, and the resulting activation maps are further refined to generate spatially complete lesion mask priors. In Stage II, a Confidence Box Matching Strategy (CBMS) is designed to integrate student predictions with Stage-I priors for generating high-confidence box prompts, which are then used to guide a SAM teacher for more reliable pseudo-label generation on unlabeled images. Moreover, a Shapley Value-Based Dynamic Weighting (SVDW) scheme is developed to adaptively balance the supervision provided by the SAM teacher and a conventional teacher during student optimization. We also conduct a preliminary plug-and-play evaluation to partially verify the extensibility of the proposed foundation model-guided priors across different semi-supervised segmentation frameworks. Extensive experiments on five medical image segmentation datasets demonstrate that FPDT-Net consistently outperforms existing semi-supervised methods and achieves state-of-the-art performance.
